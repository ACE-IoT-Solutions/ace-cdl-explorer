# G36 implementation scope

Target the applicable air-side and hot/chilled-water sequences in ASHRAE Guideline 36-2024. The four-pipe heat pump shared with a boiler and chiller needs an explicit equipment-specific sequence alongside those provisions. The current example is **G36-style**, not a verified Guideline 36 implementation.

## Current working slice

- The G36 sample uses scheduled occupied, sensor standby, unoccupied, and a six-hour preconditioning lookahead. Each VAV now has an editable local CDL thermal mode: measured room temperature relative to occupied setpoints selects warm-up or cooldown, and relative to unoccupied setpoints identifies setback or setup. Only warm-up or cooldown restores occupied temperature setpoints ahead of schedule; ventilation minimums remain reduced. The local 0–6 mode codes are teaching labels, not the OBC zone-group enumeration. Ten synthetic raw occupancy sensors are debounced by editable CDL `TrueDelay` and `Latch` blocks. Presence takes one minute to assert and five continuous minutes of vacancy to clear; saved projects with the former shipped two-minute default migrate to the corrected value. The six-hour preconditioning decision is also computed by CDL from a calendar lookahead input. The runtime now rejects a G36 graph with an undriven preconditioning or confirmed-occupancy output instead of substituting a harness decision.
- Each generated VAV has a separate airflow setpoint, measured airflow, and damper command. Its editable CDL PI compares the setpoint with the previous step’s physical airflow measurement and closes the damper when no flow is requested. Fan control uses the graph request, avoiding a low-pressure deadlock. Both samples now deliver air from graph damper and fan commands through the same simplified terminal/duct model; a request alone cannot deliver air. The G36 VAV cooling request now remains available while measured AHU supply is warm, preventing a startup loop in which a closed VAV prevents fan airflow and coil cooling. AHU temperature faults remain separate from the terminal airflow request.
- Each G36-style AHU enables a modulating mixed-air PI loop when outdoor and return temperatures permit free cooling and outdoor air exceeds an editable 12 °C teaching guard. Measured mixed-air temperature from the physical model feeds the editable CDL controller on the next step. Its minimum outside-air flow loop can raise the outdoor damper command. An editable differential dry-bulb high limit disables economizing when outdoor air reaches return temperature and re-enables below return by 1 K; raw proof of delivered fan airflow and all three freeze stages also gate the sequence. The proof is derived from the previous physical step, with no harness economizer decision. Occupied mode and fan proof enable the measured-flow minimum-air loop; outside occupied mode, CDL sets the minimum-flow setpoint to zero; a separate fan-request gate closes normal outdoor air when there is no conditioning call while permitting free cooling during cooldown or preconditioning. Both outdoor and return dampers now have independent graph outputs and editable mechanical maximums. The return command normally complements the economizer command. The first half of occupied minimum-air PI output maps over full outdoor-damper travel; the second half independently reduces the return-damper maximum. This follows the [OpenBuildingControl common-damper limit mapping](https://simulationresearch.lbl.gov/modelica/releases/v14.0.0/help/Buildings_Controls_OBC_ASHRAE_G36_AHUs_MultiZone_VAV_Economizers_Subsequences_Limits.html) for the 2020 sequence. The physical mixer calculates outside-air share from the ratio of their openings and limits fan delivery when their combined opening is below one; changing either command can change airflow, mixing, coil demand, energy, and zone temperature. This is a teaching approximation of common-damper limits. Complete G36 2024 damper sequence validation, disabled-transition delays, enthalpy options, and relief/return-fan sequences remain.
- Each G36-style VAV now sends a typed cooling supply-air reset request when its damper exceeds 95% and its cooling demand represents more than 1 K of zone error for two minutes. Each AHU sums five requests and ignores the first two, then samples a 0.1 K warm trim or up to 0.6 K cooling response every two minutes. The graph holds the weather-selected target for ten minutes after fan demand begins and otherwise resets between an editable 14 °C minimum and the outdoor-temperature upper target. Rust consumes that graph target for coil and air-delivery physics. This follows the [OpenBuildingControl supply-temperature reset](https://simulationresearch.lbl.gov/modelica/releases/v14.0.0/help/Buildings_Controls_OBC_ASHRAE_G36_AHUs_MultiZone_VAV_SetPoints.html) pattern; its request qualification and simplified operating modes are still teaching approximations.
- Both G36-style AHUs now use measured supply-air temperature in editable CDL Less/Greater, five-minute TrueDelay, and Latch blocks for Stage 1 freeze protection. Below 4.4 °C for five minutes, the graph limits outdoor air to its minimum, keeps the heating coil enabled, and sends two hot-water requests per affected AHU. Above 7 °C for five minutes, the stage clears. Connected plant CDL enables the hot-water pump and keeps its supply target at or above 45 °C during a freeze request. This follows [OpenBuildingControl Stage 1 freeze protection](https://simulationresearch.lbl.gov/modelica/releases/v14.0.0/help/Buildings_Controls_OBC_ASHRAE_G36_AHUs_MultiZone_VAV_SetPoints.html); alarm acknowledgment/suppression and equipment proof remain to be implemented.
- Each G36-style AHU now has a distinct Stage 2 freeze response. After measured supply air stays below 3.3 °C for five minutes, connected CDL blocks close outdoor air completely, expose a Level 3 ventilation-interruption alarm, and hold the shutdown for one hour using a sampled UnitDelay and timed latch-clear path. The graph then returns to Stage 1 minimum outdoor air while the supply remains below its recovery threshold. The separate Stage 3 shutdown handles longer or deeper faults.
- Stage 3 now latches if the raw freeze-stat input trips, measured supply air stays below 3.3 °C for 15 minutes, or it stays below 1 °C for five minutes. Editable CDL blocks stop the fan, close outdoor air, fully open the cooling valve, modulate the heating valve toward 27 °C using the higher of measured supply and mixed air, request hot- and chilled-water pumping, and expose a Level 2 alarm. The latch accepts an operator reset only after the freeze stat is normal and measured supply air exceeds 7 °C. The simulation scenario can inject a raw freeze-stat trip and reset pulse for either AHU; the harness does not make the shutdown decision. Fan-off coil metal heat transfer and equipment status proof are outside the simplified physical model.
- The conventional AHUs command their fixed 20% outdoor-air share through editable CDL constants. Rust reads both samples' graph outputs for the mixing box; it does not select a default damper command.
- Both AHU examples now command heating and cooling coil valve positions through editable CDL temperature-error, feedforward, PI, limiter, and mode-gating blocks. The physical model calculates coil requests from those valve commands and delivered airflow, then limits transfer by water flow and supply temperature. Mixed-air and actual supply-air temperatures return as raw feedback on the next internal step. This replaces the prior simulator-computed ideal coil request; the 20 K full-open coil model and proportional coil allocation remain simplified.
- The physical model now returns ten measured primary-airflow and discharge-temperature signals. The G36-style VAV graph uses measured flow and supply temperature to cap reheat against an editable 35 °C discharge target, and uses measured flow and discharge temperature for five-minute fault alarms. The plant still has a separate 40 °C equipment safety cap. The 15-minute sample and one-step sensor delay can overshoot the 35 °C target transiently; this is not yet a verified G36 discharge control sequence.
- Each G36-style VAV now resets an editable discharge-air temperature setpoint from the AHU supply target toward the active zone heating setpoint plus 11 K over the first half of heating demand. A CDL PI loop compares that setpoint with measured discharge temperature and commands the reheat valve; a 10 °C low-discharge exception can also enable the valve outside heating. The existing editable 35 °C cap and physical 40 °C equipment limit remain. This follows the [OpenBuildingControl VAV reheat sequence](https://simulationresearch.lbl.gov/modelica/releases/v14.0.0/help/Buildings_Controls_OBC_ASHRAE_G36_TerminalUnits_Reheat_Subsequences.html) for discharge reset and valve modulation. The graph exposes each setpoint as a Brick `Discharge_Air_Temperature_Setpoint`.
- Each VAV now holds heating airflow at its active minimum during the first half of heating demand. Above that midpoint, editable CDL blocks permit increased airflow only when measured discharge exceeds room temperature by 3 K. Preconditioning can select the heating maximum. This implements the key heating airflow interlock from the reference sequence. The new measured-airflow damper PI follows that setpoint; fully differentiated operating modes remain.
- The conventional plant retains its editable dispatch share. The G36-style plant now receives available heat-pump thermal capacity as a raw equipment input and uses editable CDL blocks to qualify hot/chilled demands with measured-flow proof, allocate the capacity across both loops, and command boiler/chiller backup for the residual. It exposes requested modes 0 off, 1 heating, 2 cooling, and 3 simultaneous service. An editable utilization parameter can restrict offered capacity; saved G36 projects migrate their former dispatch share into this parameter. Rust applies physical nameplate limits and calculates delivered heat, cooling, and energy. Under a graph-commanded simultaneous mode, its teaching physical model shares compressor power between the two services and exposes the ambient heat exchange needed to close the first-law balance (positive absorbed from ambient, negative rejected). The physical model now interpolates an editable outdoor-temperature table of nameplate capacity fraction, heating COP, and cooling COP each step. It sends the resulting available capacity to the graph before dispatch and applies the same physical limit to delivery; both modes use the interpolated COP for electricity and ambient energy balance. The bundled three-point table is synthetic and clamps beyond its endpoints. It does not vary with leaving-water temperature or part load, so manufacturer capacity/efficiency maps and calibrated simultaneous heat recovery remain future work. The [LBNL simultaneous-heating-and-cooling heat-pump model](https://simulationresearch.lbl.gov/modelica/releases/latest/help/Buildings_Fluid_HeatPumps_ModularReversible.html) illustrates the richer manufacturer data needed.
- The plant now receives raw Brick `Status` points for heat-pump, boiler, and chiller availability. Scenario outage windows change those physical statuses; CDL `Switch` blocks gate heat-pump capacity and backup commands, so loss of the heat pump transfers eligible load to boiler/chiller through the graph. The requested heat-pump mode reflects the actual CDL heat-pump command after qualification. Rust independently limits physical delivery from unavailable equipment. Saved G36 graphs migrate these gates while retaining plant tuning. This adds an unavailable-equipment test path, not full manufacturer fault, start/stop, or stage-completion feedback. Brick [describes status points as read-only equipment state](https://docs.brickschema.org/brick/timeseries), and the [OBC chiller plant model](https://simulationresearch.lbl.gov/modelica/releases/v14.0.0/help/Buildings_Controls_OBC_ASHRAE_G36_Plants_Chillers_Generic.html) distinguishes proven status from a stage command.
- The physical source model now returns raw Brick `Status` points for heat-pump, boiler, and chiller running proof on the next internal step. Editable scenario start delays defer both physical source delivery and running proof after a CDL command; an outage stops either immediately. Plant CDL compares requested stages with proven running stages, samples the confirmed stage combination, and holds hot/chilled-water resets until every requested stage is running, water flow is proven, and the editable 900-second stability timer has elapsed. A sampled CDL conversion breaks the reset-to-source feedback loop. This synthetic proof models source startup, not manufacturer start sequences, isolation-valve transitions, or detailed fault codes. The [OBC chiller stage identifier](https://simulationresearch.lbl.gov/modelica/releases/v14.0.0/help/Buildings_Controls_OBC_ASHRAE_G36_Plants_Chillers_Generic.html) likewise uses proven-on status to identify the current stage.
- Failure-to-start test windows now leave raw equipment availability true while the physical model suppresses running proof and delivery. For the heat pump, editable CDL blocks observe its prior command, require five continuous minutes without running proof, latch a start fault, and remove heat-pump capacity from dispatch so the existing boiler/chiller residual sequence serves the load. A raw operator reset pulse clears the latch for another start attempt. The fault is exposed as a Brick alarm and chart signal. Boiler and chiller now use the same editable timeout, latch, command inhibit, and independent operator reset pattern, with separate Brick alarms and chart signals. A failed boiler or chiller has no further source in this teaching plant; adding redundant equipment and staging remains future work.
- Each G36-style VAV now issues a typed integer duct-pressure request after its damper stays nearly open while measured flow remains below 90% of requested flow for two minutes. Each AHU sums its five requests and applies a ten-minute fan-start delay. Its connected CDL trim-and-respond graph subtracts two ignored requests, trims by 0.03 normalized pressure when requests are low, responds by 0.05 per excess request (capped at 0.1), and clamps the sampled target to editable 0.25–0.8 limits. A 120-second UnitDelay holds the previous sampled setpoint; a duct-pressure PI loop and airflow feedforward jointly command fan speed. This follows the shape of the G36 pressure reset but uses normalized pressure and an approximate physical duct model.
- The physical model reports delivered outside-air flow for each AHU as delivered supply flow times the opening-ratio outside-air fraction. Each VAV now calculates an editable area plus population breathing-air requirement only when both schedule and debounced presence are active; measured discharge relative to room temperature selects 1.0 cooling or 0.8 heating air-distribution effectiveness. Its AHU sums the zone requirements and measured primary flows, computes each bounded zone outdoor-air fraction, then computes system ventilation efficiency as `1 + uncorrected outdoor air / total primary air − maximum zone outdoor-air fraction`. Editable design caps bound the resulting effective outdoor-air setpoint. The existing AHU PI compares that setpoint with measured outside-air flow and can raise the outdoor and return damper commands. These are normalized-flow teaching design assumptions following the [OpenBuildingControl ASHRAE 62.1 multizone sequence](https://simulationresearch.lbl.gov/modelica/releases/v14.0.0/help/Buildings_Controls_OBC_ASHRAE_G36_AHUs_MultiZone_VAV_SetPoints_OutdoorAirFlow_ASHRAE62_1.html); the example has no calibrated floor area, population density, CO₂, window state, or ventilation compliance claim.
- Both examples expose hot/chilled supply and return water temperature and flow as raw graph inputs. Editable plant CDL enables each loop above a 0.5 kW request and commands 15–100% pump speed from load. The conventional sample sets fixed 45/7 °C supply targets. Editable hot/chilled source feedback blocks use measured supply temperature and graph setpoints to correct predicted equipment production. The conventional graph now uses four editable CDL switches to stop its heat pump, boiler, and chiller source commands when the associated loop is disabled. Rust balances source production against delivered coil and reheat load in 5,000 kg mixed volumes per loop, reports supply/return temperatures, and charges actual source and cubic pump energy. Tests verify that disabling a pump stops delivered heat/cooling, that water-side transfer matches delivered load, and that a limited source temporarily draws down stored heat.
- The G36 plant now uses measured loop flow from the previous physical step and editable CDL `Greater`, `And`, and `Switch` blocks to prove each pump before commanding its heat pump side and boiler/chiller backup. A pump starts from a CDL load or freeze request; source production begins after the physical flow measurement crosses the editable threshold. This is a basic equipment proof, not a full status, fault, or staging sequence. The runtime also requires explicit graph outputs for every G36 actuator it consumes, including both AHU dampers, and no longer falls back to an outdoor-air fraction when a G36 damper output is missing. For both examples, Rust now consumes the graph pump-speed and source-production commands directly; it no longer uses loop-enable outputs to override those commands. The loop-enable outputs remain visible for inspection.
- Both water loops now have a simplified parallel-valve and bypass hydraulic model: pump speed sets a quadratic pressure head, connected coil/reheat valve openings change network conductance, and measured remote differential pressure and flow return as raw inputs on the next step. The G36 plant graph uses the first half of each sampled reset fraction to set an editable 15–35 kPa remote pressure target, then an editable CDL PI loop and minimum-speed block command the pump. The chilled-water supply target holds at 12 °C over that first half, then falls to 7 °C over the second half through editable CDL arithmetic blocks. This follows the joint mapping in the [OpenBuildingControl chilled-water supply setpoint reference](https://simulationresearch.lbl.gov/modelica/releases/v14.0.0/help/Buildings_Controls_OBC_ASHRAE_G36_Plants_Chillers_SetPoints.html) for the 2021 sequence. Editable CDL stage detectors now combine source commands with previous-step physical running proof, then hold the exposed reset value until every requested source and measured water flow are proven for an editable 900 seconds. The delay also holds reset at startup. This approximates the reference stage hold; physical startup and status are simplified, so full staging validation remains. The hot-water counterpart and pressure values here are equipment assumptions; 2024 sequence validation and calibrated hydraulics remain. The shared hydraulic model is an equivalent resistance approximation, not a pipe or calibrated coil-valve network.
- The G36 sample computes each AHU's chilled-water reset request in plant CDL: a cooling valve above 95% (clearing below 85%) requests one; a supply-air error above 2 K for two minutes requests two; above 3 K for two minutes requests three. A five-minute sampled trim-and-respond controller ignores the first two requests, trims by 0.02 when requests are low, responds by 0.03 per excess request (capped at 0.07), and resets the chilled-water supply target from 12 to 7 °C over the upper half of the normalized reset after the lower half raises pressure. The matching hot-water path uses heating valve requests and supply-air deficits above 8 or 17 K for five minutes, then resets a 45 °C nominal target over a 44–50 °C range. Ten VAVs now add one hot-water request after their reheat valve exceeds 50% and their heating demand represents more than 1 K of zone error for five minutes. The valve hysteresis, demand threshold, delay, request sum, nominal target, and reset span are editable CDL blocks. Rust consumes both resulting targets without deciding either reset. These are teaching implementations of the [OpenBuildingControl AHU request](https://simulationresearch.lbl.gov/modelica/releases/v14.0.0/help/Buildings_Controls_OBC_ASHRAE_G36_AHUs_MultiZone_VAV_SetPoints.html) and [chilled-water plant reset](https://simulationresearch.lbl.gov/modelica/releases/v14.0.0/help/Buildings_Controls_OBC_ASHRAE_G36_Plants_Chillers_SetPoints.html) patterns. Physical actuator proof, source stage-completion feedback, and calibrated differential-pressure reset remain.

The synthetic year provides outdoor temperature, solar/internal gains, schedule, and generated presence. The physical model derives primary airflow, discharge temperature, duct pressure, mixed-air temperature, outside-air flow, and simplified water temperatures/flows/pressure from equipment delivery. CO₂, humidity, calibrated water hydraulics, and detailed actuator/proven-on feedback require richer physical models and documented design values before their controls can be evaluated. A one-node sensible zone cannot verify ventilation or latent performance.

Keep annual comparisons on the same weather, model, capacities, and timestep. The selected timestep is a maximum: editable CDL UnitDelay periods make the G36 sample use 60-second internal steps even when 15 minutes is selected. With the same equivalent water-pressure/flow physics and the default synthetic heat-pump performance table, the conventional graph records **41,214 kWh electricity, 63,755 kWh boiler fuel, and 860.48 person-present comfort exceedance zone-hours**; the G36-style graph records **56,638 kWh electricity, 46,082 kWh boiler fuel, and 4.90 zone-hours**. The conventional graph still sizes water pumps from load without remote-pressure feedback; the new valve-dependent resistance model can leave its coils short of flow. Before that hydraulic change, with speed-proportional flow and measured-flow source proof, the conventional and G36-style results were 37,314/65,040/89.2 and 38,813/73,145/5.13 respectively (electricity kWh/boiler fuel kWh/zone-hours). This large change in the conventional comfort result is a **model and control-calibration finding**, not evidence of a real G36 performance advantage. Neither pump curve, valve conductance, bypass, nor zone model is calibrated to a building. Local CDL warm-up/cooldown qualification increases the G36-style annual electricity and fuel totals from the prior 55,372/43,921 kWh while reducing occupied comfort exceedance from 5.38 to 4.90 zone-hours in this synthetic run. The G36 graph offers the heat pump its available capacity before commanding boiler/chiller residuals; its simultaneous-mode physical calculation also avoids charging heating and cooling as two independent compressors. Both changes affect this synthetic energy split. Relative to the earlier fixed-COP, fixed-capacity calculation, outdoor-temperature derating raises backup boiler use in the G36-style run; one-step physical running feedback makes a small further difference to the sampled water reset; these are model-assumption effects rather than measured equipment performance. The G36 chilled-water graph raises pressure over the first half of the sampled reset and lowers supply temperature over the second half, matching the cited 2021 OBC mapping; the applicable 2024 guideline and synthetic equipment values still need verification. No freeze-stat trip was injected. These synthetic annual differences do not establish G36 compliance or comparative energy performance.
