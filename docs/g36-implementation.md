# G36 implementation scope

Target the applicable air-side and hot/chilled-water sequences in ASHRAE Guideline 36-2024. The four-pipe heat pump shared with a boiler and chiller needs an explicit equipment-specific sequence alongside those provisions. The current example is **G36-style**, not a verified Guideline 36 implementation.

## Current working slice

- The G36 sample uses scheduled, occupied-standby, unoccupied, and six-hour preconditioning setpoints. Ten synthetic raw occupancy sensors are debounced by editable CDL `TrueDelay` and `Latch` blocks. Presence takes one minute to assert and five continuous minutes of vacancy to clear; saved projects with the former shipped two-minute default migrate to the corrected value. The six-hour preconditioning decision is also computed by CDL from a calendar lookahead input. The runtime now rejects a G36 graph with an undriven preconditioning or confirmed-occupancy output instead of substituting a harness decision.
- Each generated VAV has separate requested airflow and a pressure-affected estimate. Fan control uses the graph request, avoiding a low-pressure deadlock. The physical model returns a normalized duct-pressure measurement to the graph and no longer invents a legacy airflow request.
- Each G36-style AHU enables a modulating mixed-air PI loop when outdoor and return temperatures permit free cooling and outdoor air exceeds an editable 12 °C teaching guard. Measured mixed-air temperature from the physical model feeds the editable CDL controller on the next step; the graph sets the outdoor-air fraction. Its minimum outside-air flow loop can raise the command. The commanded share changes mixed-air temperature, coil demand, energy, and zone temperature. G36 damper limits, relief/return-fan sequences, and freeze protection remain to be implemented.
- Each G36-style VAV now sends a typed cooling supply-air reset request when its damper exceeds 95% and its cooling demand represents more than 1 K of zone error for two minutes. Each AHU sums five requests and ignores the first two, then samples a 0.1 K warm trim or up to 0.6 K cooling response every two minutes. The graph holds the weather-selected target for ten minutes after fan demand begins and otherwise resets between an editable 14 °C minimum and the outdoor-temperature upper target. Rust consumes that graph target for coil and air-delivery physics. This follows the [OpenBuildingControl supply-temperature reset](https://simulationresearch.lbl.gov/modelica/releases/v14.0.0/help/Buildings_Controls_OBC_ASHRAE_G36_AHUs_MultiZone_VAV_SetPoints.html) pattern; its request qualification and simplified operating modes are still teaching approximations.
- The conventional AHUs command their fixed 20% outdoor-air share through editable CDL constants. Rust reads both samples' graph outputs for the mixing box; it does not select a default damper command.
- Both AHU examples now command heating and cooling coil valve positions through editable CDL temperature-error, feedforward, PI, limiter, and mode-gating blocks. The physical model calculates coil requests from those valve commands and delivered airflow, then limits transfer by water flow and supply temperature. Mixed-air and actual supply-air temperatures return as raw feedback on the next internal step. This replaces the prior simulator-computed ideal coil request; the 20 K full-open coil model and proportional coil allocation remain simplified.
- The physical model now returns ten measured primary-airflow and discharge-temperature signals. The G36-style VAV graph uses measured flow and supply temperature to cap reheat against an editable 35 °C discharge target, and uses measured flow and discharge temperature for five-minute fault alarms. The plant still has a separate 40 °C equipment safety cap. The 15-minute sample and one-step sensor delay can overshoot the 35 °C target transiently; this is not yet a verified G36 discharge control sequence.
- A heating call now opens each G36-style VAV damper beyond its ventilation minimum as the editable PI demand rises; the resulting airflow request drives fan delivery. This improves person-present comfort over the fixed-minimum heating path, but still does not implement the G36 active heating flow setpoint and valve control loops.
- The physical plant caps the heat pump's combined heating/cooling command and separately caps boiler and chiller output. Both control programs use the same limits. Rust tests cover the pressure recovery, outdoor-air path, and plant shortfall.
- Each G36-style VAV now issues a typed integer duct-pressure request after its damper stays nearly open while measured flow remains below 90% of requested flow for two minutes. Each AHU sums its five requests and applies a ten-minute fan-start delay. Its connected CDL trim-and-respond graph subtracts two ignored requests, trims by 0.03 normalized pressure when requests are low, responds by 0.05 per excess request (capped at 0.1), and clamps the sampled target to editable 0.25–0.8 limits. A 120-second UnitDelay holds the previous sampled setpoint; a duct-pressure PI loop and airflow feedforward jointly command fan speed. This follows the shape of the G36 pressure reset but uses normalized pressure and an approximate physical duct model.
- The physical model reports delivered outside-air flow for each AHU as delivered supply flow times the graph's outside-air fraction. An editable AHU PI loop compares this measurement with a teaching minimum of 20% of requested airflow and can raise the damper command above the economizer selection. The minimum is not an ASHRAE 62.1 ventilation calculation.
- Both examples expose hot/chilled supply and return water temperature and flow as raw graph inputs. Editable plant CDL enables each loop above a 0.5 kW request and commands 15–100% pump speed from load. The conventional sample sets fixed 45/7 °C supply targets. Editable hot/chilled source feedback blocks use measured supply temperature and graph setpoints to correct predicted equipment production. Rust balances source production against delivered coil and reheat load in 5,000 kg mixed volumes per loop, reports supply/return temperatures, and charges actual source and cubic pump energy. Tests verify that disabling a pump stops delivered heat/cooling, that water-side transfer matches delivered load, and that a limited source temporarily draws down stored heat.
- The G36 sample computes each AHU's chilled-water reset request in plant CDL: a cooling valve above 95% (clearing below 85%) requests one; a supply-air error above 2 K for two minutes requests two; above 3 K for two minutes requests three. A five-minute sampled trim-and-respond controller ignores the first two requests, trims by 0.02 when requests are low, responds by 0.03 per excess request (capped at 0.07), and resets the supply target from 7 to 12 °C. The matching hot-water path uses heating valve requests and supply-air deficits above 8 or 17 K for five minutes, then resets a 45 °C nominal target over a 44–50 °C range. Ten VAVs now add one hot-water request after their reheat valve exceeds 50% and their heating demand represents more than 1 K of zone error for five minutes. The valve hysteresis, demand threshold, delay, request sum, nominal target, and reset span are editable CDL blocks. Rust consumes both resulting targets without deciding either reset. These are teaching implementations of the [OpenBuildingControl AHU request](https://simulationresearch.lbl.gov/modelica/releases/v14.0.0/help/Buildings_Controls_OBC_ASHRAE_G36_AHUs_MultiZone_VAV_SetPoints.html) and [chilled-water plant reset](https://simulationresearch.lbl.gov/modelica/releases/v14.0.0/help/Buildings_Controls_OBC_ASHRAE_G36_Plants_Chillers_SetPoints.html) patterns. Plant staging hold, pump proof, and differential-pressure reset remain.

The synthetic year provides outdoor temperature, solar/internal gains, schedule, and generated presence. The physical model derives primary airflow, discharge temperature, duct pressure, mixed-air temperature, outside-air flow, and simplified water temperatures/flows from equipment delivery. CO₂, humidity, calibrated water pressure and flow, and actuator/status feedback require physical models and documented design values before their controls can be evaluated. A one-node sensible zone cannot verify ventilation or latent performance.

Keep annual comparisons on the same weather, model, capacities, and timestep. The selected timestep is now a maximum: the runtime takes the greatest common divisor with editable CDL UnitDelay periods, so the G36 sample uses 60-second internal steps even when 15 minutes is selected. At 60-second steps with the mixed water volumes and proportional source feedback, the conventional sample records 60,377 kWh electricity, 100,144 kWh boiler fuel, and 0 person-present comfort exceedance zone-hours; the G36-style sample with hot and chilled-water resets, terminal reheat requests, supply-air temperature reset, and corrected five-minute vacancy delay records 41,358 kWh, 79,533 kWh, and **3.95 zone-hours**. The G36-style sample uses less energy but has a small person-present comfort shortfall under these simplified assumptions. The terminal requests raise the hot-water target when several VAVs have persistent reheat demand; cooling requests also lower the AHU supply-air target after its fan-start delay. A 40–50 °C teaching range is a configurable equipment assumption, not a G36 requirement. These differences do not establish G36 performance. Both annual summaries use the same weather, physical model, capacities, and 60-second step.
